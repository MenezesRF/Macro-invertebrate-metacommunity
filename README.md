# Macro-invertebrate-metacommunity
In this study, we compared metacommunity dynamics between an intermittent river network (IRN) and an ephemeral river network (ERN)  located in the Brazilian semi-arid region. 

**Abstract**
1. Non-perennial rivers dominate dryland landscapes worldwide, however, our understanding of the ecological processes structuring their communities is largely based on Mediterranean and temperate systems. Current metacommunity theory predicts that drying modifies the balance between local environmental filtering and regional dispersal processes by altering habitat conditions and hydrological connectivity. However, whether these predictions apply to tropical dryland rivers, where drying represents the prevailing hydrological condition rather than an episodic disturbance, remains largely unknown. Here, we compared macroinvertebrate metacommunity dynamics between an intermittent river network (IRN) and an ephemeral river network (ERN) in the Brazilian semi-arid region to evaluate how contrasting flow permanence influences biodiversity patterns and community assembly. We sampled nine sites in the ERN and eighteen sites in the IRN across four to six campaigns over one hydrological year and quantified patterns of α- and β-diversity for total macroinvertebrates, aerial dispersers, and purely aquatic dispersers. We also evaluated temporal changes in the relative importance of species sorting and dispersal limitation using partial Mantel analyses.
2. Local diversity was remarkably similar between river network types. Taxonomic richness and diversity indices did not differ significantly between the IRN and ERN, although macroinvertebrate density was substantially higher in the IRN. In contrast, the hydrological regime strongly influenced metacommunity structure. Spatial β-diversity was generally higher in the ERN than in the IRN, particularly for incidence-based (Jaccard) dissimilarity, with species turnover representing the dominant component of community differentiation. In contrast, temporal β-diversity showed limited evidence of differences between basins, with significantly higher total Jaccard dissimilarity in the ERN only for aerial dispersers and the total macroinvertebrate community. Thus, the prediction that temporal β-diversity would be lower in the ERN was not supported, although differences were restricted to total macroinvertebrates and aerial dispersers. These results indicate that differences between ERN and IRN were more consistently expressed in spatial than in temporal patterns of community dissimilarity.
3. Metacommunity assembly also differed markedly between river network types. In the IRN, the relative importance of dispersal limitation and species sorting alternated across hydrological phases. Dispersal limitation became more evident during non-flowing periods as habitat fragmentation increased, whereas species sorting was more frequently detected during flowing periods when environmental conditions differed. In contrast, the ERN showed little evidence that either environmental or geographic distances independently structured community composition, suggesting that metacommunity organization was not driven by either species sorting or dispersal limitation alone.
4. Our findings demonstrate that increasing drying severity does not necessarily reduce local diversity or strengthen deterministic environmental filtering, as predicted by current conceptual models of non-perennial river metacommunities. Although the ERN showed greater spatial β-diversity and higher total incidence-based temporal β-diversity for total and aerial communities, temporal turnover did not differ between river networks. Together with the absence of detectable signatures of either species sorting or dispersal limitation in the ERN, these results suggest that community dynamics under prolonged drying may involve stochastic processes, unmeasured ecological mechanisms, or processes operating at broader spatial or temporal scales than those captured by current metacommunity models. By revealing contrasting metacommunity dynamics between intermittent and ephemeral tropical river networks, our study extends existing theory beyond temperate systems and highlights the need to incorporate tropical dryland rivers into general models of freshwater metacommunity assembly under increasing flow intermittence.

**Keywords**
